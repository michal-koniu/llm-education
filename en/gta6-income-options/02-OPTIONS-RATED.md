## 1. How to read the ratings

### The reader this is calibrated for

- A **new entrant**: no existing GTA audience, no aged domain, no existing monetised channel.
- One person, about 3 to 4.5 hours a day for the first six weeks after launch, then 1 to 2 hours a day: about 175 to 285 hours over the first 90 days. **The probability weights below assume this commitment.** With less time, move probability down.
- A local AI rig, so model inference costs about the price of electricity. Cloud costs are added where they matter.
- Owns or can buy a current console and the game.

If you already own a monetised gaming channel with 10,000+ subscribers, or a site with search authority, move "probability" up one point on the video and web options and multiply the base case by 2 to 5 (estimate). An existing audience is the single largest advantage in this market.

### The rubric (1 to 5, 5 is always better)

| Score | Income potential (monthly, durable) | Probability of reaching base case | Agent-automation fit (within the rules) | Speed (launch window) | Risk (5 = low) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Base case ≥ $3k and high case ≥ $25k | 60% or more | Agents can do 80%+ of the hours | Revenue in the first 2 to 4 weeks after 19 Nov | Hard to lose money, accounts or legal standing |
| 4 | Upper end of base case ≥ $1.5k and high case ≥ $10k | 40% to 59% | 60% to 80% | Revenue within ~6 weeks | Small, manageable platform risk |
| 3 | High case above $5k | 25% to 39% | 40% to 60% | Revenue within ~3 months | Real policy or takedown exposure, survivable |
| 2 | High case $2k to $5k | 12% to 24% | 20% to 40% | 3 to 6 months | Serious exposure (termination, store closure, capital loss) |
| 1 | High case under $2k | Under 12% | Under 20% | Nothing in the window | Likely to be shut down or to lose money |

"Durable" means a high case that survives the year; a few viral months before a termination, or a one-off sale, do not count. "Probability" is the sum of the base and high weights in each option's table.

**Income is scored on the numbers only.** An earlier draft capped service businesses at Income 2 because their revenue scales with your own hours. That rule was dropped: it was not applied to every option, and it changed the ranking. Labour intensity shows up where it belongs, in the **Automation** score, and in the text of each service option.

"Probability of success" means the chance of reaching at least the **base** case. "Agent-automation fit" means what agents can do **while staying inside platform rules**, not what they could do technically.

### Income figures

- All figures are **net** (after platform shares, fees, hosting and tools; before income tax and before paying yourself), in USD.
- **Monthly** ranges are the **average of months 4 to 12** after launch. Months 1 to 3 are usually smaller, because partner programmes, ad networks and search engines all have lags.
- **12-month** figures are cumulative net over the first year.
- **Every income range in this file is an estimate.** It is built from the reported inputs shown next to it (view counts, RPMs, fees). No range is a reported payout.
- "Low / base / high" come with rough probability weights. The weights are judgement, not data.

## 2. Video and streaming

### V1. YouTube long-form guide channel: human-led, agent-produced

**How it pays.** YouTube ads (55% creator share on long-form) at $2 to $5 RPM; later sponsorships, gear affiliates and memberships. Rockstar's written policy bans memberships that sell access to the footage [14]. **Rule for every paid tier in this guide: sell features (tracker sync, ad-free, early tool access, community roles), never access to GTA footage or screenshots. No members-only GTA videos.**

**Evidence.** A 16-year-old GTA guide channel shows 3.19M subscribers, 1.69B lifetime views and about 3.3M views in the 28 days to 28 Oct 2026; analytics models put that month at about $13,400 (modelled) [43]. It took 9,500+ videos. GTA 6's trailers drew 93M YouTube views in 24 hours (Trailer 1, Dec 2023; Guinness lists 90.4M) and 475M across platforms in 24 hours (Trailer 2, May 2025) (reported) [1]. Demand will be far larger than any GTA V year; so will supply (see the Elden Ring channel count in file 01).

**Time to first revenue.** The Partner Program (YouTube's ad-revenue programme) needs 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 public watch hours today, and 8,000 hours for new applicants from 1 Feb 2027 (reported) [29, 30]. To use the lower bar you must **meet it and submit before 1 Feb 2027**; how applications pending on that date are treated is not documented, so check the Help page in January. At a 5-minute average view, 4,000 hours is about 48,000 views (estimate). Realistic first ad dollars: month 2 to 5.

**Where a newcomer can win.** Not the head terms ("GTA 6 walkthrough"). Narrow verticals: a single collectible family, missables and "point of no return" warnings, 100% completion routing, vehicle and weapon comparisons, settings and performance explainers, **accessibility** (colour-blind and HUD options, subtitle sizing, motion-sickness and camera settings, controller remaps, assist options, audio cues), spoiler-free "what the game doesn't tell you". Accessibility is thin on competition and poorly served by AI summaries. Speed on a niche beats breadth.

**Agents vs human.** Agents: research from your notes, script drafts, chapters, subtitles, title and thumbnail variants (from your own captures), rough cuts, Shorts candidates, upload scheduling, comment triage, analytics. Human: playing and capturing on a console (no automation possible or allowed), voice or face, spoiler decisions, fact checks, final approval. Roughly 50% to 60% of hours can move to agents (estimate).

**Sponsorships.** For mid-size guide channels, sponsor reads can match ad revenue once videos average tens of thousands of views; no GTA-specific sponsor rates were found (unverified). A paid read over GTA footage carries the same "non-commercial" ambiguity as ads, or more, and must be disclosed as advertising under consumer-protection rules. The Creator Platform and Mod Guidelines ban sponsorship **inside** servers and mods [10, 16], not in videos about the game.

**Risks.** Saturation; the Partner Program bar doubling on 1 Feb 2027; spoiler takedowns; a future change in Take-Two's tolerance of monetised footage; burn-out from capture work; being classified as "inauthentic" if the channel becomes templated.

| Case | Net / month (m4 to m12) | 12-month net | Assumptions | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low | $0 to $200 | -$500 to $1,000 | Never reaches YPP, or reaches it late at ~50k views/month | ~78% |
| Base | $400 to $1,500 | $2,000 to $10,000 | YPP by month 3 to 5; 150k to 400k views/month at $3 to $4 RPM; ~$100 of affiliate/sponsor | ~18% |
| High | $5,000 to $15,000 | $35,000 to $110,000 | Top-3 channel in a GTA 6 vertical; 1.5M to 3M views/month at $4 to $5 RPM plus sponsors | ~4% |

**Scores:** Income 4 (borderline: the base case touches the $1.5k line) · Probability 2 · Automation 3 · Speed 3 · Risk 3.

### V2. Shorts, TikTok and Reels as a standalone business

**How it pays.** Shorts pool (45% share) at $0.03 to $0.20 per 1,000 views; TikTok Creator Rewards for 60-second-plus videos (reported) [45]. From 1 Feb 2027, Shorts pool payouts need 10M qualified views per 90 days (reported) [30].

**Evidence.** 50M Shorts views at $0.07 RPM is about $3,500 (estimate). AI-faked GTA 6 clips reached millions of views and attracted DMCA strikes (reported) [22, 23].

**Agents vs human.** The most automatable video format: cut selection, captions, hooks, multi-language captions, scheduling and A/B tests. Human: spoiler gate, capture, final review.

**Verdict.** Excellent as a **feeder** for V1 and W2. Weak as a business on its own: the per-view money is tiny and supply is unlimited.

### V3. Faceless, AI-voiced, high-volume channel networks

**How it pays.** Same as V1, but templated, synthetic narration, many uploads, often many channels.

**Why it rates badly.** This is the exact profile YouTube's "inauthentic content" policy describes [28]. Large AI-slop channels were removed in Jan 2026 [32]; a cluster-detection system reportedly terminated about 130,000 channels in six months, judged by shared templates and infrastructure, with under 1% reversed according to one newsletter summary (unverified) [33]. Running ten channels from one rig, one script template and one voice is the pattern it is designed to find (estimate). For GTA 6 specifically, AI-generated "gameplay" is what Take-Two has been striking [22].

**The legal version is V1.** AI-assisted work with a human's capture, editorial angle and real narration remains eligible. A **single** channel with a synthetic voice and genuinely original, human-captured, human-edited guides sits in between; disclose AI use and expect lower reach.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low | -$100 to $0 | -$1,000 to $0 | Rejected from YPP or terminated | ~90% |
| Base | $0 to $300 | $0 to $2,000 | Monetised with limited ads or reduced reach | ~9% |
| High | $2,000 to $6,000 until terminated | $5,000 to $30,000 | A few viral months, then demonetised | ~1% |

**Scores:** Income 1 (the high case is not durable) · Probability 1 · Automation 3 (the 80%+ automated version breaks the rules, so it does not count) · Speed 3 · Risk 1. The mass-produced multi-channel version is listed in `05-EXCLUDED.md`.

### V4. Live streaming (Twitch, Kick, YouTube Live)

**How it pays.** Subscriptions (Twitch 50/50 for most; Kick 95/5), ads, tips, sponsors, plus VOD and highlight revenue (reported) [36].

**Evidence.** GTA V's 2B+ hours a year (2024) are mostly RP on FiveM, carried by a few hundred entertainers (reported) [40]. In Feb 2020, 17 of about 285,000 GTA streamers had reached 1M hours in 90 days (reported) [41]. RDR2's launch week drew half the Twitch hours of a multiplayer shooter (reported) [62].

**Agents vs human.** Agents: clipping, VOD chapters, titles, social posts, moderation helpers, translated VOD captions. Human: the performance, which is the product. AI-run "streamers" exist; no evidence was found that they work for GTA, and they add audience-trust and platform-rule risk (unverified).

## 3. Web, data and community

### W1. Editorial guide and news site (text-first)

**How it pays.** Display ads; small affiliate; sponsorships.

**Evidence.** A 13-language GTA site network reported 1.49M visitors and 11.8M page views in 30 days to 16 Sep 2026, 54% from Google (reported, own media kit; "visitors" is summed across 13 domains and may double-count) [47]. That network is more than ten years old. Google's AI Overviews cut clicks on factual queries roughly in half (reported) [38]. Game-guide answers ("where is X") are short facts, the most exposed format.

**Where it fails.** Head terms belong to publishers with review copies; a new domain waits 3 to 6 months for search trust (estimate); text answers are the easiest thing for an AI summary to replace. Agents can draft, translate, interlink and monitor, but without first-hand play and human fact checks the site becomes the "scaled content abuse" Google's policy describes [37].

### W2. Interactive map and collectible tracker (data-first web app)

**What it is.** A web app built on **structured data**: every collectible, vehicle spawn, property, mission, missable and weapon as a database record with coordinates, screenshots you captured, conditions and verification status. Users tick items off; progress is saved locally for free and synced to an account for a small subscription. Every record also renders as a crawlable page.

**How it pays.** Display ads (light, so as not to wreck the tool); an ad-free synced tier (for example $2 to $4 a month, estimate) that sells features, never access to screenshots or footage; sponsorships, disclosed; later a data API and embeds (see W7).

**Evidence.** A single-purpose GTA V map site gets about 300k visits a month with 34-second visits (reported, analytics tools) [48, 49]: the traffic is real, the ad value per visit is low. A large GTA reference database shows "gta 6 map" as its top keyword (reported) [48]. A leading multi-game interactive-map business shows a global rank of about #7,000 (reported) [48]. A community-run wiki for a 2023 launch shows 11M to 12M visits a month two years later (estimate) [66].

**Why this is the best web option for an agent-heavy operator (estimate).**
1. A tool with saved progress cannot be replaced by an AI Overview. The answer engine can tell you where one item is; it cannot track your 87 out of 100.
2. Structured data is where agents shine: ingest, deduplicate, validate, render, translate, monitor.
3. The game cannot be datamined on console. Data must come from play. **Community submissions** (via Discord, see W5) beat a publisher's two reviewers within days if you build the intake well.
4. It compounds into the 2027 second wave: PC and an online mode will add new categories (mod locations, online businesses) to the same schema.

**Where it fails.** Big interactive-map businesses will ship a GTA 6 map fast. You win only on depth in a niche, better UX (spoiler-free mode, missables, filters, languages), accuracy and community. Wrong data kills trust immediately. The map base layer must be **drawn from your own observations**, not traced from the in-game map or official art: a close tracing is arguably a derivative work, and even an original drawing remains a takedown risk. Never ship ripped game assets; carry a not-affiliated disclaimer (estimate; no takedowns of GTA V map sites were found, but survivorship bias applies). Keep "GTA" and "Grand Theft Auto" out of the site, domain and any companion-app name and icon; descriptive use in page text is normal.

**Agents vs human.** Agents: 70% to 80% of hours (estimate) — schema, build, deploy, intake moderation, duplicate detection, coordinate conversion, page rendering, translation, SEO monitoring, uptime. Human: in-game verification of each new item type, sampling of community submissions, spoiler policy, UX taste.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low | $0 to $150 | -$1,000 to $800 | Under 30k sessions/month by month 12 | ~76% |
| Base | $200 to $2,400 | $1,500 to $15,000 | Months 4 to 12 average: 80k to 250k sessions/month × $3 to $8 per 1,000 = $240 to $2,000 gross ads, less 10% to 25% network share = $180 to $1,800; plus 0.05% to 0.2% of monthly users (≈ half of sessions) at $3 = $60 to $750; less hosting $50 to $200 → **≈ $190 to $2,350** | ~19% |

### W3. Localised editions (multiplier on V1 and W2)

**How it pays.** More page views and more viewers for assets you already own.

**Evidence.** In the 13-language GTA network above, 27.4% of page views were non-English: Spanish 987k, Chinese 525k, Indonesian 465k, Portuguese 464k, French 296k a month (reported/derived) [47]. 21% of its visitors came from Latin America (reported) [47]. YouTube now auto-dubs into up to 20 languages for free [35], so video translation is no longer a moat; **web** localisation still is.

**Where it fails.** Non-English audiences skew to lower-RPM countries. Mass unreviewed machine translation is the "translating" example in Google's spam policy [37]. Use your own wording, not long runs of the game's localised in-game text.

**Rule of thumb (estimate).** Start with a language you speak natively, if it has a GTA audience: you can publish without paid reviewers, and smaller languages (the same network shows Turkish 122k, Polish 118k and Ukrainian 60k page views a month [47]) have thin local competition, though lower ad RPM. Then grow to 6 to 8 languages where the GTA audience is large and local competition thin: Spanish, Portuguese (Brazil), French, German, Polish, Turkish, Indonesian. Have a native speaker review a sample of every batch.

| Case | Effect | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Low | Adds ~$0 (no parent asset) | — |
| Base | Adds 10% to 25% to the parent's revenue: on a base-case W2 + V1 ($600 to $3,900) that is **~$60 to $1,000/month** | ~32% given a working parent |
| High | A Spanish or Portuguese edition outgrows the English one: +$2,000 to $6,000 | ~3% |

**Scores:** Income 3 · Probability 3 · Automation 5 · Speed 3 · Risk 3. Cannot stand alone; income and probability are conditional on a working parent asset.

## 4. Services

### S1. Creator services (B2B): subtitles, translation, thumbnails, Shorts cuts, editing

**How it pays.** Monthly retainers or per-video fees from mid-size GTA creators who will be overloaded in the launch window. Example pricing (estimate): $100 to $300 a month per creator for subtitles in 5 languages plus Shorts cuts.

**Why it works for an agent rig.** Your pipeline (file 03) already produces these outputs; selling the capacity turns agent leverage into cash without your own audience. Elden Ring had 183,670 channels streaming in 3.5 days (reported) [54]; many GTA 6 creators will need help.

**Where it fails.** Sales is the bottleneck, and cold-DM spam gets you banned from communities. Client scoping, communication and acceptance are human work, which is why Automation is 3, not 4. Revenue grows with clients served, so the high case means hiring or an agency. YouTube's free auto-dub erodes the translation part [35]. Margins fall as every editor adopts the same tools. Never reuse a client's footage or data without a written agreement.

**Evidence.** FiveM: peaks of about 220k to 300k concurrent players, a mid-2026 30-day average near 126,000 (reported, methods differ) [53]. In Tebex's creator-code dataset across game webstores (about 1.5M transactions, mostly mobile games, **not** FiveM script sellers), the top 1.6% of creators took 75.9% of spend (reported) [51, 86]. Weak evidence for P3, but it fits every other creator market measured: extremely top-heavy.

**Why it rates well on automation.** Lua and JavaScript scripts are where coding agents are strongest: write, test on a local server, document, build the store page, answer first-line support. The human sets the product strategy, polishes UX and holds quality. Script piracy is endemic; use the platform's asset escrow.

**Why it matters for GTA 6.** If GTA 6 gets a Creator Platform in 2027 to 2028, it will almost certainly run through Cfx and Tebex [10, 16] (estimate). A reputation and a storefront built now is the best preparation.

### C2. Console resale arbitrage

**Facts.** PS5 Pro sold out at US retailers by 31 Aug 2026, with resale listings at $1,200 to $1,300 against an $899.99 list price (reported, one snapshot) [72, 74]. In the 2020 PS5 launch, resale peaked near $1,000 on a $499 console, then fell about 30% below launch-era prices within a year (reported) [73]. GTA 6 itself is not scarce: digital copies are unlimited and boxed copies are download codes [4].

**Economics (estimate).** €150 to €300 gross per unit at the peak, about €50 to €200 net after marketplace fees (8% to 13%), shipping and returns; zero or negative after a restock. Ten units tie up about €9,000 for a possible €500 to €2,000 one-off.

**Rules.** Reselling a lawfully bought console is generally legal in the EU (estimate; check your country). Retailers limit quantities and cancel orders. Repeated buy-to-resell is a business activity for tax purposes. Under the EU's DAC7 rules, marketplaces report sellers with 30 or more sales or over €2,000 a year, per platform, to tax authorities (reported) [75]. Automated checkout bots are excluded (file 05).

**Scores:** Income 1 (a one-off, not monthly or durable) · Probability 2 · Automation 2 · Speed 5 · Risk 2. Capital-hungry, time-boxed and not an agent business.

**Scores:** Income 1 · Probability 2 · Automation 3 · Speed 2 · Risk 4. Base: $0 to $200 a month.

**The selection rule.** An unweighted sum rewards safe, small, fast options, so it is not used. The brief asks for big net income, especially where agents do most of the work:

1. Keep options with **Income ≥ 3 and Automation ≥ 3** (a real ceiling, and agent leverage inside the rules).
2. Drop options with **Risk ≤ 2** or **Speed 1**. Set aside multipliers and add-ons that cannot stand alone (W3, W7, C1).
3. Rank by probability, then income, then automation. Break remaining ties on launch-window fit.

**The result.** S1 and S3 lead on probability (3). They tie on every score; S1 goes first because it is launch-timed and reuses the same agent pipeline. W2 leads the probability-2 group on income (4) and automation (4). **So the top three are S1, S3 and W2.** V1 is fourth: it ties W2 on income but scores lower on automation. P3 is fifth.

**What that means.** The rule says something an earlier draft hid behind an unstated scoring cap: **the most likely income is selling agent-boosted services** (S1, S3), whose revenue grows with clients and hours and tops out around a small agency. **The biggest ceilings with agent leverage are W2 and V1**, at roughly a 1-in-4 or 1-in-5 chance of reaching their base case. The recommended combination in `03-AGENT-PIPELINES.md` uses both: W2 and V1 for upside, and S1 (or S3 if you code) for cash. W3 multiplies W2 and V1. V2 and W5 are feeders.

### The honest headline

No option combines high income, high probability and high automation. The options with the biggest ceilings (W2, V1, P3) have a 22% to 24% chance of reaching their base case, and V4, P4 and P1 only about 10%. That is because the GTA economy is a power law: most of the money goes to a small top tier that existed before launch. The options with better odds (S1, S2, S3) are services: more reliable, but bounded by sales and by your time. Agents lower your cost per output, but they lower everyone else's too, which is why automation alone does not move probability.
