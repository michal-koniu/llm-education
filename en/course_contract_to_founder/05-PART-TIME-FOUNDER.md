# Part 05 · Operating as a part-time founder

> **Goal of this part.** You build a weekly operating system that lets you run the startup next to the
> contract for 12–18 months without breaching the contract, without spending all your time building
> instead of selling, and without burning out. You learn what the evidence says about part-time founders,
> and you put a price on the one resource you are shortest of: daytime hours with customers.
>
> **Time:** ~3 h + a 4-week trial · **Level:** ⭐⭐⭐ · **Produces:** `founder/05-week.md`,
> `founder/05-review.md`

---

## 0. The problem

Your customers — lawyers, notaries, accountants — work 9:00 to 17:00 and meet during those hours. Your contract
may expect you to be available during exactly the same hours (part 01, §2). Your best building time is the
evening and early morning, when nobody can be interviewed. And building is what you are good at and enjoy, so it
will quietly take every free hour unless something stops it.

The result, for most technical founders, is a beautiful product shown to nobody. This part is the something that
stops it.

---

## 1. What the evidence says about part-time founders

Two views, both serious:

| View | Evidence |
| :-- | :-- |
| **"Go all in."** Commitment is a signal and a necessity | Paul Graham, *How Not to Die* (2007): *"The number one thing not to do is other things."* Y Combinator expects founders to *"commit to working full-time … during the batch and afterwards"* (YC FAQ). Antler calls its residency *"a full-time commitment"*. Investors quoted by LaunchVic call a founder who is not full-time a *"red flag"* |
| **"Start hybrid."** Keeping income first improves survival | Raffiee & Feng (*Academy of Management Journal*, 2014): entrepreneurs who start **while keeping their paid work** have a **33.3% lower hazard of exiting** their business than those who quit first |

They do not contradict each other. They describe two different stages:

- **Validation and first pilots** (stages 0–1 of part 04): you are looking for the truth about a market. Keeping the
  contract lowers the pressure to lie to yourself, funds the search, and the survival data favours you.
- **Scaling and fundraising** (stage 2 and beyond): the work exceeds what fits around a contract, and investors
  read part-time as lack of conviction. Part 06 sets the conditions for crossing from the first to the second.

I found **no explicit statement from a Polish or CEE investor** requiring founders to be full-time, and no survey on
how they view founders who keep a consulting contract. Field experiment 06 asks them directly.

## 2. The hours, by kind

Not all startup hours are the same. Split them by **when they can happen**:

| Kind of work | When it can happen | Weekly target, stage 0 | Weekly target, stage 1 |
| :-- | :-- | --: | --: |
| **Customer time** — interviews, demos, pilots, partner meetings | business hours (sometimes 8:00 or after 17:00) | 3–5 h | 4–8 h |
| **Building** — agents, evaluation sets, the box image | any time — evenings, early mornings, weekends | 5–8 h | 8–12 h |
| **Business admin** — accounting, contracts, e-mail | any time, low energy | 1–2 h | 2–3 h |
| **Learning** — this business track | any time | 2–3 h | 1–2 h |

Rule for stage 0: **customer time is at least a third of startup hours.** If it is less, you are building a product
for an imaginary customer (pitfall B21).

---

## 3. The price of a customer day

The hard constraint is customer time during business hours. You have three ways to get it:

1. **Edges of the day.** Many professionals will meet at 8:00 or 17:00–18:00, before or after court and clients.
   Ask for those slots first. Online meetings make this much easier.
2. **Contract hours, if the contract allows.** Only if part 02 showed that your availability terms permit it — and
   never during hours you report as worked for the bank.
3. **An unpaid day off from the contract.** On a B2B contract you can usually take a day without invoicing it —
   check the contract and agree it with the client in advance like any day off.

Option 3 has a clear price: **PLN 1,700 per day**. That makes it a business decision, not a sacrifice. Two customer
days a month cost PLN 3,400 — less than a single pilot box, and you can fit 4–6 conversations or two pilot
installations into one well-planned day in one city. Write it into the stage budget of part 04 as a line:
*"customer days: 2 per month × PLN 1,700"*.

## 4. Leverage: use your own agents

You have spent ~150 hours learning to build agents that work while you do not. Use them on the startup — and treat
it as dogfooding of the box:

| Job | Agent pattern from your courses | Note |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Market research, competitor monitoring, price changes | the **Researcher** (`course_agentic_apps`, network through a proxy, strict sandbox) | public sources only |
| Interview notes: transcription, summary, coding of pains and prices | the **Archivist** (local documents, no network) | **interview notes contain information about real offices — they stay local.** Exactly the promise you will make to customers; live by it first |
| Follow-ups, reminders, weekly review preparation | the **Steward** (calendar, reminders) | |
| Drafting proposals from the price book | a local model with the price book as context | a human reads every proposal before it is sent |

This does three things: it saves hours, it gives you real operating experience of your own product category, and it
produces honest demo material that contains no customer data.

## 5. The weekly and monthly rhythm

### 5.1 The weekly review — 30 minutes, same time every week

`founder/05-review.md`, one section per week:

```markdown
## Week 2026-W46
Customer conversations held: _   (ladder level reached: 1:_ 2:_ 3:_ 4:_)
Startup hours: customer _ / build _ / admin _ / learning _
Spend this week: _ PLN (stage budget left: _ PLN)
Gate status (part 04): _ of _ conditions met
Contract: all hours delivered? any conflict? (yes/no + note)
Next week: 3 customer actions, 1 build goal
Energy (1–5): _   Days fully off: _
```

The contract line matters. The day you notice the startup creeping into contract time is the day to fix it — not the
day the client notices.

### 5.2 The monthly gate review

Once a month, compare the last four weekly reviews with the gate and the stop condition of part 04. Three possible
results, written down:

- **Continue** — the gate is on track.
- **Change** — something specific changes: the profession, the agent, the price, the channel.
- **Stop** — the stop condition is met. Stopping on schedule is a success of the method, not a failure of you.

## 6. Energy

Two years of contract plus startup is the most common way founders in your position burn out. The rules that protect
you are boring and non-negotiable:

1. **One day a week fully off** — no contract, no startup. Logged in the weekly review.
2. **A hard stop in the evening** — building after a fixed hour costs tomorrow's customer conversation.
3. **The people at home know** — the plan, the budget, the stop condition. A startup kept secret from your family is
   paid for twice.
4. **Warning signs** in the review: energy at 1–2 for two weeks in a row; no day off for two weeks; contract work
   slipping. Any one of these → the next week has half the startup hours.

## 8. ⭐ FIELD EXPERIMENT 05 — four weeks on the operating system

**Hypothesis.** Running the week of exercise 05 for four weeks yields at least 8 customer conversations with no conflict
with the contract and at least one full day off every week.

**Procedure.** Run the week exactly as designed. Fill the weekly review every week. Take one unpaid contract day for
customers in the four weeks (if the contract allows it and the client agrees in advance). At the end, compute the
totals.

## 9. Pitfalls

**B21 · Building instead of selling.**
*Symptom:* 12 hours of building and 1 hour with customers this week. *Remedy:* customer time ≥ ⅓ of startup hours in
stage 0; the weekly review shows it.

**B22 · Startup work in contract standby time.**
*Symptom:* a customer call at 11:00 while you are "available" to the bank. *Why it hurts:* a breach, and a
reclassification indicator either way. *Remedy:* edges of the day, or an agreed unpaid day.

**B23 · No weekly review.**
*Symptom:* "I know roughly where I am." *Remedy:* 30 minutes, same slot, written. The gates of part 04 need it.

**B24 · Hero mode.**
*Symptom:* no day off for three weeks because "momentum". *Remedy:* the rules of §6; half the hours next week.

**B25 · Private data in cloud tools — your own.**
*Symptom:* interview notes about real offices pasted into a cloud chat to summarise. *Why it hurts:* you are selling the
opposite. *Remedy:* the Archivist pattern, local only.
