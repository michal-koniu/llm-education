## The thesis the whole course stands on

> **Your contract is not the obstacle to the startup. It is the startup's first investor — and the cheapest
> one you will ever have.**

It pays about PLN 1,700 per day, leaves you most of the day, and asks for no shares. The ways to lose that investor
are specific: breach a clause, mix IP, mix taxes, let the startup creep into contract hours, or quit on a good week.
Every part of this course closes one of them. The research behind the course also found evidence for the thesis:
founders who start while keeping paid work have a one-third lower hazard of exiting their business (Raffiee & Feng,
2014). It also found the limit: investors expect full-time founders from the round onwards. Part 06 sets the moment
you cross from one to the other.

## The rules I keep throughout the course

1. **Your contract, not a typical contract.** Every legal point is a question to check against **your** text.
2. **Every number has a label:** measured, researched (with source), computed for this course, or assumed.
3. **Decide in advance.** Caps, gates, stop conditions and quit triggers are written before you need them.
4. **Nothing from the bank enters the startup.** Not code, data, documents or "patterns".
5. **The `founder/` folder is private.** Never in the public `llm-education` repository (B5).
6. **Pitfalls are numbered across the business track:** **B1–B30 here**, B31–B70 in the pricing course.
